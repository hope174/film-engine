SYNTAX: HOW EACH LINE SHOULD LOOK

This file shows how every kind of line in a script should look.
Examples are indented. "Right" shows a line that works. "Wrong" shows a common
mistake, with the reason after it, so you know what to look out for.
For a longer explanation of each part, see Script guide.txt.
To see every word you can use in brackets, run:  filmengine\run.bat words


THE SHAPE OF A SCRIPT

A script goes in this order. Only the cast and at least one spoken line are needed.
1. Notes and the title.
2. The cast: who is in the film and which voice each one has.
3. Everything that happens, from the start to the end, one thing on each line.

Example:
    # The Lighthouse, written by Mara's biggest fan
    Title: The Lighthouse
    Narrator = Ryan
    Mara = Sonia
    [ambience: waves]
    Narrator: The boat reached the rocks at midnight.
    Mara: Hello?
    [stop all, fade out 3]


NOTES

A note starts with a hash sign. The engine skips it.
Right:
    # This scene needs more thunder.
Wrong:
    Note to self, add thunder
    (There is no hash sign, so the engine thinks someone is speaking.)


SETTINGS

A setting is a word, a colon, then a value. Seconds are written as plain numbers.
Right:
    Title: The Lighthouse
    Gap: 0.5
    Ending: 4
    Format: mp3, ogg
    Scene: The Kitchen
Wrong:
    Gap: half a second
    (Write the number in digits.)


THE CAST

Name, equals sign, voice. Styles can follow the voice, after commas.
Right:
    Narrator = Ryan
    Mara = en-GB-SoniaNeural
    Old Man = Guy, low, slow
    Radio Host = Aria, radio, rate +10%
    Screen reader = Microsoft David, rate +20%
Wrong, and the engine will not warn you:
    Narrator: Ryan
    (A colon means someone is speaking, so the narrator would say the word Ryan out loud.
    The cast uses an equals sign.)
Wrong:
    Mara = Sonia, whisper, happy
    (happy is not a style the engine knows. Run the words list to see the styles.)
Wrong:
    Mara = Sonia whisper
    (Put a comma between the voice and each style.)


SPOKEN LINES

Name, colon, words. The name must be in the cast, spelt the same way.
Capital letters in the name do not matter.
Right:
    Mara: Hello? Is anyone there?
    mara: Capital letters in the name do not matter.
Wrong:
    Marra: Hello?
    (Marra is not in the cast. The engine will ask if you meant Mara.)
Wrong:
    Hello? Is anyone there?
    (Nobody is named, so the engine does not know who says it.)

A long line can carry on to the next line of the file, with no empty line between.
Right:
    Narrator: The lamp had been dark for three days,
        and nobody in the village would say why.


HOW A LINE IS SAID

Put styles in round brackets, between the name and the colon. Separate them with commas.
Right:
    Mara (whisper): Did you hear that?
    Mara (whisper, echo): Did you hear that?
    Keeper (low, slow, behind): I have been waiting.
    Keeper (volume 60, rate -10%): I have been waiting.
    Mara (on the phone): I'm outside.
Wrong, and the engine will not warn you:
    Mara: (whisper) Did you hear that?
    (The brackets must come before the colon. After the colon, the word whisper is spoken out loud.)
Wrong:
    Mara [whisper]: Did you hear that?
    (Use round brackets for styles. Square brackets are for sounds.)


SOUNDS

Square brackets, the word sound, a colon, then the sound's name. Options follow, after commas.
Use the file's name without the ending: door-slam.wav is written as door-slam.
Right:
    [sound: knock]
    [sound: knock, left]
    [sound: thunder, nowait]
    [sound: thunder, nowait, delay 1.5, behind right]
    [sound: footsteps, move left to right, volume 70]
    [sound: phone, length 3, fade out 1]
Wrong:
    [sound knock]
    (There must be a colon after the word sound.)
Wrong:
    [sound: knock
    (The closing square bracket is missing.)


MUSIC AND BACKGROUND SOUNDS

The same shape as a sound, but with music, ambience or background in front.
They keep playing, repeating, until they are stopped.
Right:
    [music: drone, fade in 3]
    [music: drone, volume 40, no duck]
    [music: chime, once]
    [ambience: rain]
    [ambience: wind, volume -6 db, fade in 4]
    [background: fire, left]


STOPPING

Right:
    [stop music]
    [stop ambience, fade out 4]
    [stop all]
    [stop all, fade out 5]
Wrong:
    [music stop]
    (The word stop comes first.)


A SOUND THAT LOOPS

Add loop to a sound. Stop it with its own name.
Right:
    [sound: clock, loop]
    [stop clock]


PAUSES

Right:
    [pause 2]
    [pause 0.5]
    [pause: 3 seconds]
Wrong:
    [pause two]
    (Write the number in digits.)


SPEAKING AT THE SAME TIME

Add overlap to the second line. It starts at the same moment as the line before it.
Right:
    Mara: Run!
    Keeper (overlap): Get down!


SCENES

Right:
    Scene: The Lighthouse Stairs
    [scene: The Lighthouse Stairs]


A WHOLE SHORT FILM

This is a complete script that works. Copy it into a new film's script.txt to try it.
Example:
    Title: The Visitor
    Narrator = Ryan
    Mara = Sonia
    Stranger = Guy, low

    Scene: The Front Door
    [ambience: rain, fade in 2]
    [music: drone, volume 40, fade in 4]
    Narrator: Mara was alone in the house when the knocking began.
    [sound: knock, front left]
    Mara (whisper): Who's there?
    [pause 1.5]
    [sound: knock, front left, volume 120]
    Stranger (muffled, front left): Let me in. It's cold out here.
    Mara (loud): Go away!
    [sound: thunder, nowait, behind right]
    Stranger (overlap, muffled, front left): Please.
    [stop music, fade out 2]
    [pause 1]
    [sound: footsteps, going away]
    Narrator: And then, the footsteps went away.
    [stop all, fade out 4]
